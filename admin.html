                    Milestones

                    When a reader reaches a milestone (an hour of reading, five books, a bingo line…), a celebration pops up on their page, once: the badge, your message, and a short video, audio clip or picture if you add one. A reward adds bonus minutes, just like a bonus at the Desk. Readers who are already past a milestone when you add it or turn it on get it the next time they open their page. Earned badges stay on their page. The starter milestones are off: edit them and turn on the ones you want.

                      Video, audio and picture files

                      • Formats: MP4 or WebM video, MP3 or M4A audio, JPG, PNG, WebP or GIF pictures. Up to 50 MB each. iPhone videos (.mov) need saving as MP4 first.
                      • Space: the free Supabase plan has 1 GB of file storage for the whole project, shared with book covers. Keep clips short: 15 to 45 seconds is plenty. Record or export at 720p, not 4K: a 30-second 720p clip is usually 5–15 MB. Audio is small (about 1 MB a minute as MP3).
                      • Words: every file needs the words that go with it (what’s said, or what the picture shows), for kids who can’t hear or see it or are somewhere quiet. Kids always press play themselves.
                      • Public: anyone with the link can watch a file, so only upload what you’re happy to share, with permission from everyone in it (and their parents for children).

                          Each board has its own dates, squares and bonuses: a day, a long weekend, a week, a fortnight or the whole summer. One board runs on any day (NZ dates); gaps between boards are fine. Kids can only tick the board running today. Finished boards stay, read-only, so kids can look back at how they went.

                            New announcement

                            Announcements

                              Books kids find through search are skipped if their title has a blocked word, and reviews with one are flagged “Rude words” for you to check. Words are matched at the start of a word (so “fucking” is caught), and short words only as a whole word, so “Sussex” is fine. Look-alikes such as sh1t, f.u.c.k and macron tricks are caught too.

                                Kimi’s stops

                                Kimi walks from the first stop to the last, in this order. Kids see each stop’s name, emoji and info sections when they tap it, on their page, the sign-in page and the TV. Changes show there within a few minutes. Hide a stop to take it off the route but keep it for later.

                                  Route

                                  Faded pins are hidden stops (not on the route). The red line is how far Kimi has walked so far. Click a pin to edit it.

                                  Kimi’s story

                                  The little book kids see once on their first visit, and any time from the “Kimi’s story” button: up to 8 pages, each with an emoji, a picture or Kimi’s drawing. Each stop can also have a story chapter and Kimi’s find (a real New Zealand critter Kimi sniffs out there): edit a stop above to change them. They unlock for kids as Kimi reaches each stop, with a “Kimi found…!” moment once per reader, and fill up “Kimi’s journey so far” and Kimi’s bug collection. The TV shows Kimi’s latest find.

                                    Wording

                                    Every word kids see on their pages and the TV. Change any of it, in English or te reo Māori, and kids see it the next time a page loads. Reset puts the site’s own wording back. Bingo boards, news, events, badges and stops are changed in their own tabs.

                                    Translation spreadsheet

                                    A spreadsheet of everything kids and the TV can show: all the wording below, plus the bingo boards, news, events, badges, Kimi’s stops, bonus reasons, prize names and where events are. Just two columns: the English, and the te reo Māori (filled in where there is some already). Give it to a fluent speaker: it opens in Excel, Google Sheets or Numbers, with a “How to” tab that explains everything. Then upload it here (Excel or CSV). Each row is matched by its English, and you’ll see every change before anything is saved.

                                    Kimi’s Kāpiti Island climb

                                    Every kid climbs their own Kimi up Kāpiti Island with their own minutes: from the beach at Rangatira, where the boat lands, up the Wilkinson Track to the top of Tūteremoana (521 m), then back to the beach for the next climb. Kids see how many times they’ve climbed it and how far up this climb Kimi is, on the island in 3D.

                                    Climbing pace

                                    Set it either way: change one number and the other follows. Decimals are fine. The one you change is the one that’s kept.

                                    How kids are doing

                                    Before launch: Kāpiti Island is of great significance to mana whenua (Ngāti Toa Rangatira, Te Āti Awa ki Whakarongotai and others). Check with mana whenua and the library that they’re happy with Kimi’s climb to Tūteremoana. The track is from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) and the island’s shape from LINZ’s NZ 8m elevation model (CC BY 3.0 NZ); both are credited on every island view.
